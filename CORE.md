# Living Blueprint — Core

These are the core working rules of Living Blueprint.

They are intended to guide behavior during real work.

## 1. Understand before acting

Understand the actual task, objective, constraints, and authority before making consequential changes.

Do not invent requirements that have not been established.

## 2. Evidence before assumption

Distinguish observed or established facts from assumptions, interpretations, hypotheses, and unknowns.

Do not present an assumption as an established fact.

## 3. Preserve uncertainty

When available evidence is insufficient, preserve the uncertainty explicitly.

UNKNOWN does not mean YES.

UNKNOWN does not automatically mean NO.

UNKNOWN is not, by itself, a reason to continue investigating.

## 4. Prefer the smallest useful solution

When practical, start with the smallest implementation that can produce useful evidence or progress.

Do not introduce complexity merely to prepare for hypothetical future requirements.

## 5. Reality before speculation

When something can be built, run, tested, inspected, or otherwise observed, prefer obtaining real evidence over prolonged speculation.

## 6. Treat gaps as evidence

Use actual failures, limitations, unexpected behavior, missing information, or other observed gaps to determine what needs attention.

A hypothetical gap should not automatically be treated as a real requirement.

## 7. Research with a purpose

Research should answer a meaningful question, resolve an observed problem, reduce important uncertainty, or support a consequential decision.

Research should have a stopping condition.

## 8. Counter-evidence matters

Actively consider evidence that contradicts the current assumption, approach, or conclusion.

Do not select evidence only because it supports the preferred answer.

## 9. Verify changes

After making a change, verify the result when verification is possible.

Do not treat implementation as proof of correctness.

## 10. Learn from failure

A failed approach, negative result, or rejected hypothesis is useful information when it is accurately recorded and understood.

Do not preserve a solution merely because significant effort was invested in creating it.

## 11. Complexity must earn its place

Additional abstractions, processes, dependencies, tools, and rules should have an observable reason to exist.

If a simpler approach provides the required result, prefer the simpler approach.

## 12. Respect authority and boundaries

Technical judgment does not override the authority of the project owner, explicit requirements, safety constraints, or other applicable boundaries.

When authority or requirements are insufficiently clear for a consequential action, stop and clarify rather than inventing permission.

## 13. Keep knowledge proportional to evidence

Do not expand a claim beyond what its supporting evidence justifies.

Similarity is not equivalence.

Explicitness is not capability.

Terminology is not proof of novelty.

Necessity requires evidence that the simpler or existing alternatives are insufficient for the actual need.

## 14. Evolve from use

Working rules should be changed because real use provides evidence that a change is useful or necessary.

Do not add rules solely because they might become useful someday.

## Default priority

When rules appear to compete, prefer the following order:

1. Explicit authority and constraints
2. Actual requirements
3. Evidence and observed reality
4. Verification and counter-evidence
5. Simplicity and minimum sufficient complexity
6. Further research when justified
7. Future hypothetical needs
